The 33% 1-octanol avoidance assay was performed as described previously. Briefly, a drop of odorant solution was placed near the nose of a forward-moving animal and the latency to initiate a reversal was recorded. Animals that failed to respond within 20 s were classified as non-responders (NR) and included in all analyses censored at 20 s. Four conditions were tested in N2, cest-2.1 (PHX3900), and tbh-1 (RB1161): animals removed from food 3 min prior to testing with 33% 1-octanol; the same 3-min starvation condition with buffer only (no odorant); animals removed from food 20 min prior to testing with 33% 1-octanol; and the 20-min starvation condition with 10% 2-nonanone. OP50 refers to the bacterial strain on which animals were previously maintained and does not indicate food presence during the assay.

Statistical comparisons were performed using a log-normal linear mixed-effects model (LMM) fit in R (lme4, lmerTest) of the form log(response time) ~ genotype + (1|date) + (1|recording_id), with pairwise contrasts against N2 estimated via emmeans and p-values adjusted by the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. The date random intercept accounts for day-to-day variation in assay sensitivity; the recording_id random intercept (one level per genotype–date plate) accounts for within-day plate-to-plate variation. If the full model yielded a singular fit, the recording_id term was dropped and the model was re-fit with date as the only random effect. For display, bars show the mean of per-day medians ± SEM across days, treating the experimental day as the unit of replication. Individual animal response times are shown as translucent dots; NR animals at the 20 s ceiling are shown as open circles; per-day medians are shown as filled black dots. Significance indicators (*, **, ***) correspond to BH-adjusted p < 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, respectively, from the LMM contrasts vs N2.
