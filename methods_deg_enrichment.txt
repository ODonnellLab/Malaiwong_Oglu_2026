To assess whether genes differentially expressed in cest-2.1 mutants (DEGs) are enriched for CAN neuron-expressed and CAN-specific genes, we performed a hypergeometric enrichment test across two single-cell transcriptomic atlases: the Taylor et al. adult and L4 C. elegans neuron atlases, and the Ghaddar et al. (2023) adult C. elegans whole-organism atlas.

For each atlas, the background universe (N) comprised all genes with at least one non-missing expression measurement. CAN specificity was quantified as the fold enrichment over mean neuronal expression: for each gene, the mean TPM was computed across all cell types in the atlas, treating non-detected values (NaN) as zero. This denominator includes all neurons regardless of expression level, so that a gene expressed at high levels in many neurons has a high mean and thus a lower fold value for CAN, correctly reflecting lower specificity. For each fold threshold (fold_thr ∈ {0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4×, 8×, 16×, 32×, 64×}), the gene pool K was defined as genes satisfying both CAN_TPM ≥ 1 TPM and CAN_TPM / gene_mean ≥ fold_thr. The cest-2.1 DEG list (n = 25 L4 DEGs or n = 40 adult DEGs from Taylor et al., derived from Taylor_L4_GenesExpressing-BATCH-thrs2_DEGfiltered.csv and Taylor_Adult_GenesExpressing-BATCH-thrs2_DEGfiltered.csv) was intersected with the atlas universe; k denotes the number of DEGs satisfying both conditions. Enrichment was calculated as (k/n) / (K/N). Statistical significance was assessed using a one-tailed hypergeometric test (upper tail). To control the false discovery rate, p-values were corrected using the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) procedure applied per neuron across its 9 fold thresholds. Filled markers in the figure indicate q < 0.05. Gene pool sizes below K = 5 are not plotted. The maximum theoretical fold value for a gene exclusively expressed in CAN across 132 cell types (Taylor L4) is 132×.

The same test was applied to all other cat-1-expressing neurons (ADE, ADF, AVM, CEP, HSN, NSM, PDE, RIC, RIH, RIM, RIR, ASI, VC_4_5) to assess specificity of any enrichment to CAN. A fourth panel compares CAN against all 132 Taylor L4 neuron types.

For the Ghaddar atlas, which covers 180 cell types including non-neuronal tissues (intestine, body wall muscle, hypodermis, gonad, pharynx), we restricted the cell type set to 104 types with names overlapping the Taylor neuron atlases: 88 exact name matches plus 16 merged Ghaddar cell types (CEP_ADE_PDE, DA_VA, DB_VB, VD_DD, PHA_PHB, PLM_ALM, PVD_FLP, URX_AQR_PQR, SDQ, AWC, ASE, IL1, IL2, RMD, RME, PLN) whose constituent neurons are present in Taylor. This restriction ensures that the gene_mean denominator reflects neuronal expression rather than non-neuronal tissues. Ghaddar gene identifiers use the standard 8-digit WormBase format (e.g. WBGene00021160); Taylor identifiers carry a trailing version digit (e.g. WBGene000211602) that was stripped before cross-atlas matching. Gene name cross-referencing confirmed that apparent name discrepancies (e.g. scb-1 in Taylor mapping to H19N07.3 in Ghaddar) reflect WormBase annotation version differences rather than incorrect gene assignments. One gene in the adult DEG list, Y17D7C.3 (WBGene000453662), is annotated as a pseudogene and was excluded from all analyses; it is absent from the Ghaddar atlas. The final adult DEG count is 40; the L4 DEG count is 25.

All analyses were performed in Python using pandas, numpy, and scipy.stats.hypergeom. Full numerical results are provided in data/deg_enrichment_can_statistics.csv (CAN only) and data/deg_enrichment_all_neurons_statistics.csv (all cat-1+ neurons).
